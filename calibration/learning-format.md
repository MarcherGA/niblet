# Learning-format calibration

## Goal

Design a music-learning system Itay will start consistently, sustain, remember, and apply to song production and vocal pitch/note identification. Do not assign a fixed visual/auditory/kinesthetic label. Calibrate delivery medium, pacing, stimulation, feedback, accountability, sustainable task size, and measured retention through questions plus real prototype trials.

## Question 7

**Which mechanism mattered most to maintaining your 800+ day streak?**

1. Not wanting to lose the number already built
2. The reminder reduced the effort of deciding when to practice
3. Seeing progress, levels, animations, and completion feedback
4. The daily task was small enough that starting felt painless

**Answer:** All of them. Itay specifically wants to discover the right daily task/lesson size that can be sustained consistently.

**Working implication:** Use a combined system: streak preservation, a fixed cue/reminder, visible progression and satisfying completion feedback, plus a deliberately small required core. Test whether an optional continuation layer permits deeper learning without making the daily start feel heavy.

## Question 8

**What daily core would you realistically start even on a low-energy day?**

Options ranged from a 90-second micro-lesson through a naturally expanding lesson.

**Answer:** Elements of all options appeal, but start by testing a substantial **7–10 minute lesson**.

**Working implication:** Do not equate sustainability with making all content tiny. Test a 7–10 minute guided experience with an immediate start, a meaningful event in the first 60–90 seconds, continuous pacing, a satisfying completion point, and optional deeper practice afterward.

## Question 9

**Which video style would most likely hold attention for the full lesson?**

1. Energetic on-camera presenter
2. Narrated animation
3. DAW/screen demonstration
4. Polished hybrid

**Answer:** Narrated animation currently seems most promising. Presenter-led and especially DAW/screen demonstrations also work and may fit certain concepts better. Itay needs real examples before deciding.

**Working implication:** Use narrated animation as the provisional default, DAW capture for production-native concepts, and presenter/embodied footage where human demonstration adds meaning. Validate with competing samples that teach the same concept rather than relying on preference alone.

## Question 10

**How should active participation be placed inside the lesson?**

**Answer:** Probably a very short required tap, prediction, imitation, or answer every 60–90 seconds, but uncertain.

**Working implication:** Do not lock this in. A/B test two otherwise-matched samples: one with brief required checkpoints every 60–90 seconds and one with continuous narration plus optional non-blocking activities. Compare completion, annoyance/friction, immediate recall, and next-day recall.

## Question 11

**Which concept should the competing video prototypes teach?**

Options included sung-note matching, interval recognition, major/minor affect, and translating a hummed melody into a DAW.

**Answer:** All are valuable; the purpose and meaning of competing prototypes needed clarification.

**Decision:** Preserve all four as future curriculum tracks. Use sung-note matching/sharp-versus-flat as the controlled prototype topic because it directly supports a primary learner goal and naturally exercises animation, audio, voice, visual feedback, and DAW/tuner demonstrations. First compare narrated animation against DAW/screen presentation with script and timing held constant; then compare required versus optional participation using the winning presentation style.
